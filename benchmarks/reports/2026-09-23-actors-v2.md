## What each mode measures

- **single** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-throughput.scala`, N=1000000): 1 sender thread x 1 actor: the per-actor pipeline floor. The single-method invariant serialises one actor, so more workers cannot help.
- **fan-out** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-fanout.scala`, N=1000000): 1 sender thread x 1000 actors: ready-queue stress.
- **MPSC** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-mpsc.scala`, N=1000000): 4 OS producer threads -> 1 actor: sender contention on one mailbox.
- **MPMC** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-mpmc.scala`, N=1000000): 4 OS producer threads x 4 actors, round-robin.
- **ping-pong** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-pingpong.scala`, N=100000): ask/reply latency through the cooperative suspension: every round trip is two messages, one frame snapshot and one resume.
- **await** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-await.scala`, N=100000): 100 caller actors awaiting one shared echo actor. Must complete at every worker count, including 1.
- **priority** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-priority.scala`, N=200000): a Low-band flood plus 1000 timed High-band asks; the p50/p99 below are the High-band ask latencies measured with System.nanoTime.

## Reading of this run (added by hand, 2026-09-23)

DESIGN §1 says protoScala is not a fast Scala; no runtime change was made to
improve a number here, and none of the observations below is a conclusion
about *why* — only about what the interleaved, spread-reported measurement
shows.

- Every one of the 42 protoScala cells and 24 protoClojure cells passed
  verification (right message count, right `Actor.stats`, `ok` on the last
  line, 5-for-5 samples). No cell was estimated or is missing.
- The load average climbed from 2.96 at the start to 9.51 at the midpoint
  and 9.68 at the end — this machine has no quiet window, and the run itself
  (two multi-threaded runtimes' worth of actor workers) is part of what
  drives that number up. The interleaving is what keeps the comparison
  meaningful despite the climb: every (mode, workers) pair samples
  protoScala and protoClojure back to back, so a load spike lands on both
  columns, not on whichever one happened to run first.
- **The protoClojure gap reproduces.** protoScala runs at 0.11x-0.83x
  protoClojure's median rate across the four comparable shapes (was
  0.10x-0.89x in the superseded single-sample run) — i.e. protoClojure is
  1.2x-9x faster depending on mode, consistent with the earlier run's
  reading of protoScala running at roughly a fifth to four-fifths of
  protoClojure's rate on the same shapes.
- **`fan-out` getting worse with more workers reproduces, clearly.**
  protoScala's median falls from 104,384 msg/s at w=1 to 56,475 msg/s at
  w=16 (-46%), while protoClojure's rises from 314,711 to 507,122 msg/s
  (+61%) over the same range. The spreads at every worker count are narrow
  relative to the trend (e.g. protoScala w=16: [55,332-56,912], a 3% band
  against a 46% drop from w=1), so this is a real, repeatable shape
  difference, not sampling noise. It is the largest and most consistent
  divergence in the table.
- `single` and `MPSC` are flat-to-declining with more workers for
  protoScala, as in the superseded run; `MPMC` is again the only mode where
  protoScala's own numbers improve with workers, peaking at w=4
  (254,412 msg/s, 1.47x its w=1 rate) before flattening past the physical
  core count (6).
- **protoScala's mailboxes are still the CAS-list fallback**
  (`--version` reports `actor mailboxes: CAS list`; protoCore's
  `ProtoMPSCQueue` has not merged into this protoScala checkout). That the
  CAS-list mailbox's push-rebuild-and-retry cost, and its per-turn
  `hasWork`/`highestPendingBand` scan across three bands, are *why*
  `fan-out` regresses with 1000 actors under contention is a **plausible
  but UNCONFIRMED hypothesis** — no code change or profiling was done in
  this measurement to isolate the mailbox as the cause, and no other
  candidate (e.g. ready-queue contention, GC pressure from 1000 live actor
  objects) was ruled out. It is recorded as the first thing to re-measure
  once `ProtoMPSCQueue` lands, not as an established explanation.
- High-band ask p50 stays at 27-42 µs under a 200k-message Low-band flood
  across all worker counts, matching the superseded run's reading that the
  priority bands do what they claim; the p99 of ~2.5-2.9 ms is the tail of
  a loaded host, not a scheduler failure (both flood and asks completed
  and verified at every worker count).
