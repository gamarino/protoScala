## What each mode measures

- **single** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-throughput.scala`, N=1000000): 1 sender thread x 1 actor: the per-actor pipeline floor. The single-method invariant serialises one actor, so more workers cannot help.
- **fan-out** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-fanout.scala`, N=1000000): 1 sender thread x 1000 actors: ready-queue stress.
- **MPSC** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-mpsc.scala`, N=1000000): 4 OS producer threads -> 1 actor: sender contention on one mailbox.
- **MPMC** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-mpmc.scala`, N=1000000): 4 OS producer threads x 4 actors, round-robin.
- **ping-pong** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-pingpong.scala`, N=100000): ask/reply latency through the cooperative suspension: every round trip is two messages, one frame snapshot and one resume.
- **await** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-await.scala`, N=100000): 100 caller actors awaiting one shared echo actor. Must complete at every worker count, including 1.
- **priority** (`benchmarks/actors/actor-priority.scala`, N=200000): a Low-band flood plus 1000 timed High-band asks; the p50/p99 below are the High-band ask latencies measured with System.nanoTime.

## Reading of this run (added by hand, 2026-09-23)

These are the **first** actor numbers protoScala has ever produced, measured on
a shared machine whose load average went from 3.59 to 9.09 during the run, with
the **CAS'd-`ProtoList` mailbox fallback**, not protoCore's `ProtoMPSCQueue`.
Nothing below was tuned for: DESIGN §1 says protoScala is not a fast Scala, and
no runtime change was made to improve a number.

- Every one of the 42 cells passed verification: the right message count, the
  right `Actor.stats`, `ok` on the last line. No cell was estimated.
- `await` completes at **every** worker count, including 1 (20,969 msg/s) —
  the point of the mode. A blocking `await` would deadlock there.
- `MPMC` is the only mode that scales, peaking at 4 workers (250k msg/s, 1.4×
  the one-worker rate) and flattening above the physical core count, which is
  protoST's measured worker-scaling ceiling.
- `single` is flat-to-declining with more workers, as expected: the
  single-method invariant serialises one actor, so extra workers only add
  spin-before-park CPU on an already busy host.
- **`fan-out` regresses with more workers** (97k at 1 worker down to 54k at 8),
  where protoClojure improves (307k → 526k). That is the clearest signal in the
  table and it is a real cost, not noise: protoScala's mailbox here is the
  fallback, whose push rebuilds a `ProtoList` under a compare-and-swap and
  retries under contention, and whose `hasWork`/`highestPendingBand` scan reads
  all three bands of an actor at the end of every turn. With 1000 actors and
  several workers, those two costs meet. The first thing to measure again after
  Phase P2 merges `ProtoMPSCQueue` is this row. It is recorded, not fixed here.
- Against protoClojure on the same host and day, protoScala runs at 0.10×–0.89×
  its rate. protoClojure's actors carry raw C++ message nodes; protoScala's
  carry protoCore objects the collector traces, which is the P1 cost the design
  accepts deliberately (DESIGN §8.4 lists it as the correction protoScala makes
  to protoClojure's model).
- High-band p50 stays at 30–42 µs under a 200k-message Low-band flood, so the
  bands do what they claim. The p99 of ~2.6 ms is the tail of a loaded host.
